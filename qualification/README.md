Welcome to the qualification round of the Fizmat AI Olympiad 2024. It opens with two warm-ups and then runs through ten short programming problems wrapped in a space-station story — from counting and probability to geometry, matrices, and a little regression. Each task is scored independently; solve them in any order. You submit a program that reads from standard input and prints the answer. Good luck.

- **Warm-up 1 — Choosing a Rocket.** Count the ways to choose k rocket parts out of n (combinations).
- **Warm-up 2 — Expected Value.** Compute an expected value and print it as a fraction.
- **A — Flight Speed.** The rocket's distance-vs-time graph is a parabola; report its speed at a given time (the derivative).
- **B — Flight Speed 2: Data Lost.** Reconstruct the parabola `a x² + b x + c` from three surviving points.
- **C — Ray is Behaving Bad.** Find the probability that a random walk on the station's staircase graph reaches the top floor in three minutes.
- **D — Gravitational Matrix.** Multiply two matrices.
- **E — Into Space.** Given six pairwise distances in some order, decide whether they can be the distances among four points in the plane (yes/no).
- **F — Meteorites with Gold.** A short closed-form computation from two integers.
- **G — Ray Is Misbehaving 2: Interference.** Fit a line to noisy (area, price) readings and flag the meteorites that deviate.
- **H — Dividing the Stars.** Draw a line through one neutron star and one giant star so that each half-plane holds equally many neutron and giant stars.
